The first fight lasted only 26 seconds: Van caught a kick, Pantoja injured his arm or elbow on the fall, and the title changed hands. This rematch is the real test. Van brings historic striking volume and youth; Pantoja brings the division’s deepest championship grappling résumé.

The hinge: Taira controlled Van on the mat before Van rallied late in their title fight. Pantoja is the more proven scramble fighter, but he returns from surgery and a nine-month layoff. Van’s reported lingering knee issue matters for a fighter who needs open-space footwork.

Guide call: Pantoja at plus money, narrowly. The market is split and Van is the community favorite, so this is a high-information, low-confidence pick—not a guarantee.

Four pressure points

What is most likely to decide the week’s UFC card.

The title fight finally starts

Van’s 26-second injury win left the sporting question open. This time, volume striking meets Pantoja’s scramble pressure over five scheduled rounds.

Ruffy’s power vs. Arman’s floor

Ruffy has 13 knockouts in 14 wins, but Tsarukyan owns the exact wrestling blueprint that produced Ruffy’s lone UFC loss.

Crossroads everywhere

Vera has lost four straight, Tuivasa seven, Chikadze three; Ortega debuts at 155 after dangerous featherweight cuts; Pitbull returns at 39 after facial fractures.

Prospect stress tests

Baraniewski has never seen round two. Steveson has never left round one. Menifield and Sharaf exist to make the hyped favorites answer a new question.
