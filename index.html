The big read

Fight-week snapshot from Sept. 17. Odds can move. Friday weigh-in results were not available when this guide was researched.

8.43 SLpMVan’s significant-strike pace
2.79 TD/15Pantoja’s takedown rate

The first fight never became a real fight: Van caught a kick and Pantoja dislocated his elbow while bracing the fall, ending the bout in 26 seconds. This rematch finally answers the matchup question.

The hinge: Van’s pace and power can drown opponents late, but Taira banked early wrestling rounds before Van rallied. Pantoja’s scrambling is at least as dangerous. If the champion keeps it in open space, his volume is difficult to oppose; if Pantoja creates repeat mat exchanges, the plus-money side has a real structural path.

Guide lean: Pantoja at +110, narrowly. The injury ending proved little, and his grappling directly attacks Van’s clearest visible vulnerability. Van remains the deserved favorite on youth, pace and improved takedown defense.

Four pressure points

The tactical questions most likely to decide Saturday.

The rematch finally starts

Van’s 26-second injury win left the sporting question open. This time, his volume boxing meets Pantoja’s scramble pressure over five scheduled rounds.

Ruffy’s power vs. Arman’s floor

Ruffy punishes hesitation, but Tsarukyan owns the same committed-wrestling profile that froze Ruffy against Benoit Saint Denis.

Prospects meet resistance

Baraniewski has never needed round two. Steveson remains raw at 4–0. Menifield and Sharaf exist to make the favorites answer new questions.

Crossroads on the prelims

Vera’s output has declined, Tuivasa carries a seven-fight skid, and Chikadze has one win in roughly four and a half years. Each faces pressure built to expose that decline.

Late change: Brian Ortega withdrew with an eye injury on Monday, Sept. 14. Renato Moicano was rebooked against Tom Nolan for UFC Vegas 123 on Oct. 31. There is no Moicano–Ortega fight on UFC 331, and no replacement bout was added.
